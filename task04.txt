### 5. Comparison operator

The finance team wants to identify expensive products.

> Display all products whose price is **greater than ₹1,500**.
 SELECT name FROM Products WHERE price > 1500;
---

### 6. BETWEEN

The marketing team wants products in the mid-range price segment.

> Display products whose price is **between ₹500 and ₹2,000**.

---SELECT name FROM Products WHERE Price between 500 AND 2000 ;

### 7. IN operator

The company is running a campaign for three categories:

* Electronic
* Clothing
* Shoes

> Display products belonging to any of these three categories.

---SELECT NAME FROM Products WHERE Category IN ('Electronics' , 'Clothing' , 'Shoes');

### 8. LIKE operator

The marketing team wants to find products based on their names.

> Display products whose product name **starts with "S"**.

---SELECT * FROM Products WHERE name Like 's%';

### 9. DISTINCT

The manager asks:

> "What different product categories do we currently have?"

Display each category **only once**.

---SELECT DISTINCT (Category) FROM Products;

### 10. ORDER BY

The manager wants to see the most expensive products first.

> Display all products from **highest price to lowest price**.

---select name from Products ORDER BY Price ASC;

### 11. LIMIT + ORDER BY

The manager now says:

> "I only want to see the **5 most expensive products**."

Write one query to produce this result.

---SELECT name FROM Products ORDER BY DESC LIMIT 5 ;

### 12. Alias

The reporting team wants a cleaner output.

Display:

* `product_name`
* `price`
* `quantity`

But rename the output columns as:

* `Product`
* `Price_INR`
* `Stock`

Use aliases.

---SELECT name AS 'Product'  , price AS 'Price_INR' , Quantity as 'Stock' FROM Products;

### 13. COUNT

The company wants to know:

> "How many products are currently stored in our product catalogue?"

Use an aggregate function.

---SELECT Count(name) FROM Products;

### 14. Multiple aggregate functions

The finance team wants a basic price summary.

Find:

* highest product price
* lowest product price
* average product price
* total of all product prices

Use appropriate aggregate functions.

---SELECT MAX(Price) , MIN(Price) , AVG(Price) , sum(price) FROM Products;



### 15. Aggregate + filter

The company wants to analyse only relatively expensive products.

> Find the **average price** of products whose price is greater than ₹1,000.

---SELECT avg(Price) From Products WHERE Price > 1000;

### 17. Date filtering

The company wants to analyse recently launched products.

> Display all products launched **after January 1, 2025**.

Use the appropriate date condition.

### 18. Boolean filtering

The inventory manager wants to see only products that are currently available.

> Display products where `is_available` is TRUE.

---SELECT name from products where is_available = true;
